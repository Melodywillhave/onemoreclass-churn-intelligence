# Service Recovery Playbook

## Purpose

Service problems should be addressed before promotional retention activities when service friction is a major churn-risk driver.

## Service Friction Signals

Relevant signals may include:
- Refund requests.
- Customer complaints.
- Low issue-resolution rates.
- Long resolution times.
- Repeated customer service interactions.
- Low satisfaction scores.

## Priority Service Recovery

When service friction is the primary risk driver:

1. Review recent customer service interactions.
2. Identify unresolved issues.
3. Prioritize outstanding cases.
4. Review recent refund requests.
5. Escalate cases with unusually long resolution times.
6. Resolve the service problem before sending promotional retention messaging.

## Escalation Guidelines

Manual escalation should be considered when:
- A refund request is active.
- Multiple customer interactions remain unresolved.
- Average resolution time exceeds 48 hours.
- Customer satisfaction is materially low.
- The issue cannot be resolved through standard support procedures.

## Messaging Guidance

Service recovery communication should:
- Acknowledge the customer's issue.
- Focus on resolution.
- Avoid promotional language before the issue is addressed.
- Avoid promising refunds, credits, or compensation unless authorized.
